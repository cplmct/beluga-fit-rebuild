## Content quality requirements

Write concise, practical, modern coaching content that is easy to scan inside the existing coaching sheet.

For each exercise include:

### What it is

Explain:

- The general movement pattern.
- The main muscles or regions involved without claiming complete isolation.
- The equipment or setup.
- The kind of user or goal the exercise may suit.

Use wording such as “primarily trains,” “places relatively more emphasis on,” or “requires.”

### Setup

Explain:

- Starting position.
- Stance, grip, or body alignment.
- Equipment position.
- Bracing and stability.
- A useful default setup while acknowledging individual adjustment.

Do not state that one exact stance, grip, or joint angle is mandatory for everyone.

### Perform the movement

Explain:

- The movement sequence.
- The intended direction of force.
- A controlled range of motion.
- How to finish and return to the starting position.
- How to maintain control rather than chase range or load.

### Breathing

Explain:

- When to inhale.
- When to exhale.
- How bracing may be used for heavier efforts.
- That users should not hold their breath indefinitely.
- That breathing can be adjusted for the exercise, load, and individual needs.

### Coaching cues

Provide approximately three to six short cues, such as:

- “Control the lowering.”
- “Keep the load close.”
- “Move through the whole foot.”
- “Stay tall through the crown of the head.”

Avoid excessive cueing that makes the movement confusing.

### Common mistakes

Include realistic errors such as:

- Excessive momentum.
- Losing a stable position.
- Using a range of motion the user cannot control.
- Letting the load drift.
- Increasing load before technique is repeatable.
- Forcing a painful position.

Do not describe normal individual differences as automatically incorrect.

### Variations

Include approximately three to five variations per exercise.

For every variation provide:

- Name.
- Purpose.
- Form change.
- Difficulty: easier, similar, or harder.

Use a mixture of:

- Easier or beginner variations.
- Equipment substitutions.
- Grip or stance changes.
- Stability changes.
- Range-of-motion modifications.
- Tempo or pause variations.
- More challenging progressions.

Do not claim a variation completely isolates a muscle. Explain relative emphasis or changed demands.

### Safety notes

Include:

- A controlled-load recommendation.
- A reminder to use a range of motion the user can control.
- A reminder to stop or modify for sharp pain, numbness, dizziness, or concerning symptoms.
- A general educational disclaimer that the content is not medical advice.
- A recommendation to seek qualified professional guidance where appropriate.

Avoid fear-based language and avoid diagnosing injuries.

## Content consistency

Use the same tone and level of detail as the existing five records.

Avoid:

- Absolute “perfect form” claims.
- Guaranteed injury-prevention claims.
- Claims that a movement is inherently dangerous or inherently safe for everyone.
- Unsupported medical claims.
- Overly prescriptive body measurements.
- Copying text from external websites.
- Copyrighted or unattributed exercise-library wording.

Write original content based on accepted resistance-training principles.
